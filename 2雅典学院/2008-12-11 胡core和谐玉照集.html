    胡core和谐玉照集


    In 2004, Hu ascended to the Chairmanship of the Central Military Commission, thus solidifying his portfolio（职位） of power. He had been President of the People's Republic since 2003 and General Secretary of the Communist Party since 2002


    A native of Jiangsu Province, Hu's rise began in northwestern Gansu, where, in 1982, he was promoted to Secretary of the Provincial Communist Youth League


    Hu (left) is considered part of the "fourth generation" of Chinese leaders. He was preceded in the job of Communist Party General Secretary by Deng Xiaoping (second from right) and Jiang Zemin (between them). In this photo, the three men meet with Party delegates in 1992


    Hu's ascent was greatly enhanced by his appointment in 1988 to Party Chief of the restive Tibet Autonomous Region, where he oversaw a political crackdown the following year. In this photograph he stands next to the Panchen Lama, a figure appointed by the Chinese to head the government of Tibet in the absence of the Dalai Lama


    In 1992, the senior leaders of the Chinese Communist Party tapped（指定、选定） Hu (center) to join the supremely powerful Politburo, making him the youngest member of the seven-member committee


    Hu (grey tie, second from left, front row) joins the other top Chinese leaders for a 1999 event marking the restitution（归还） of Macau to China


    Under Hu's leadership, China has expanded its influence in Africa through investment and trade. In this photo, he arrives for a two-day visit to Mozambique


    In 2005, Hu played host to President Bush in Beijing. The summit did little to alleviate（減輕;緩和） tensions between the two nations over the status of Taiwan, China's record on human rights and its swelling trade surplus


    The Chinese leader attends an APEC leaders retreat in 2006


    The Chinese President arrives at a diplomatic meeting in Hanoi


    Hu will preside over the first China's first Olympic Games in the summer of 2008
