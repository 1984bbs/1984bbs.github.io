                  The eldest son of the Chinese president Hu Jintao faces questioning in connection with a multi-million pound corruption investigation in Namibia.

                  By Sebastien Berger Southern Africa Correspondent And Malcolm Moore In Shanghai
                  Published: 5:25PM BST 17 Jul 2009
                  Three people have been arrested in the country on charges of fraud, corruption and bribery involving a government contract with the state-owned Chinese company Nuctech, a world leader in scanning technology.
                  Hu Haifeng, 38, was the president of the firm until last year, when he was promoted to being the party secretary of Tsinghua Holdings, the group which controls Nuctech and 30 other companies.

                              欧盟也正在调查威shi

                              European Union probes Chinese 'soft loans’ ploy over NucTech

                              A company linked to the eldest son of the Chinese president, Hu Jintao, is under investigation by the European Union after being accused of "illegal dumping" by Smiths Group, one of the UK's largest engineering companies.

                              NucTech is owned by the Chinese government and is under the control of Hu Haifeng, the low-profile 38-year-old son of China's leader.

                              It is one of the world's leading makers of x-ray scanners for airports and ports and supplies every Chinese airport with security devices. In Europe, however, it has been accused of plying customers with "soft loans" from the Chinese government to pay for its machines, effectively reducing their price and undercutting Smiths, its main competitor.

                              Smiths claimed NucTech was using the enormous resources of the Chinese government to lower prices allegedly "to a degree that precludes free and open competition." It claimed the market had become "fatally distorted" and urged the European Commission to "act firmly and restore commercial credibility" to the market. The Commission said it was in the middle of a "full investigation".

                              It is unclear whether Mr Hu has been formally questioned by the Commission, but he has recently resigned his directorship of NucTech although he is the Communist Party secretary of a holding company that oversees NucTech, alongside a number of other companies.

                              NucTech signed a contract with HM Revenue & Customs in 2006 to supply its machines to British ports. It also held talks with BAA, which operates several major airports, but no deal was disclosed for security reasons. The investigation by the EU, which was launched in March, has coincided with another investigation into alleged corruption in Namibia, which also allegedly received a soft loan from China in order to buy Nuctech machines.

                              Paulus Noah, the director of Namibia's Anti-Corruption Commission, told The Daily Telegraph last week that he would like to question the younger Hu.
                              Nuctech was also recently probed in the Philippines after winning an airport security contract before it had been put to public tender.

                              Smiths Group, along with Science Applications International, a US engineering group, is one of Nuctech's main rivals for the lucrative business of providing security scanners to airports and ports.

                              The disputes could also spiral into a global embarrassment for the Chinese government, which has come under increasing scrutiny for the rampant corruption that has allegedly blossomed under its leadership.
